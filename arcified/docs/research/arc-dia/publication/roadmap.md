Build an excellent desktop browser on Linux, Windows and macOS. Use Arc's useful workflows, Dia-informed performance research and YAB inspiration, with improvements preferred over literal replication. Keep proprietary code and assets out of the implementation.

## Priority and scope decisions

The owner does not need cross-device sync. The six sync proposals and their tracker are closed as not planned. Local export/import, durable sessions and organization across windows on the same device remain useful and in scope. The browser requires no Arc account or new Arcified account.

Prioritize the 135 active non-AI work items ahead of the 23 later AI/supporting items. This includes native UX, Linux/Windows/macOS parity, measured core performance and the other accepted browser features. AI is optional and later; its consent and security requirements still apply if implemented. No core item is blocked on AI work.

Optional external accounts are limited to chosen integrations such as GitHub Live Folders, calendars, remote AI providers and any approved hosted-sharing service. These do not introduce an Arc account requirement. Core browsing, local boards and portable sharing/import/export work without them.

## Published scope

164 recorded leaf work items: 158 active and 6 excluded. There are 12 active topic trackers and one excluded sync tracker, plus this roadmap, for 178 historical issues: 171 open and 7 closed as not planned. Active work remains proposed or unverified. The active catalog contains 137 implementation items, 13 decisions, 6 research items and 2 evidence trackers. The native dependency graph has 262 blocking relationships and no catalog cycles.

Implementation, verified behavior and released behavior are separate states. Optional candidates can be approved or deferred through their decision issues. Publication does not approve every optional service, provider connection or automation capability. Ordinary browsing remains local-first without a required account or assistant.

## Improvements over the references

- Offer top tabs and either sidebar orientation over the same state model.
- Explain Temporary, Kept in this Space and Profile shortcut consequences, with safe Profile ownership transitions.
- Make command verbs, source provenance and destinations visible. Opening the command field creates no empty tab or implicit assistant action.
- Provide an ownership organizer, reversible grouped operations, additive repeatable migration and optional/pausable cleanup.
- Use explicit focus routing across windows, transient views and split panes, with keyboard, accessibility, IME and platform-native behavior.
- Keep portable local data and account-free browsing. Use browser-owned consent receipts for any optional assistant, integration or tool capability.
- Require measurements on declared native hardware. Inspect and reuse Helium features before adding another implementation.
