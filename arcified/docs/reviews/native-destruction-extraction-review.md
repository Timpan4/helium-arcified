## Prioritized remaining gates

1. **P1, already declared: construction-time native loss has no safe continuation or cleanup contract.** Patch lines 42 to 61 issue native completion without distinguishing a constructing owner. Retained `Browser::Create()` at [browser.cc][browser], lines 233 to 247, adds manager ownership only after construction. Consequently manager deletion during construction finds no entry. Views widget.cc (local uncommitted `arcified/native/patches/window_lifetime/inputs/ui/views/widget/widget.cc`), line 600, calls `InitNativeWidget()` and continues accessing native/delegate state; initialization is marked complete only at line 660. BrowserWidget's retained initialization at lines 220 to 238 continues after `Init()` and dereferences `browser_native_widget_`, which the native-destroy callback clears. The [factory][factory], lines 61 to 84, and Browser constructor, lines 318 to 334, then continue with native access, session `WindowOpened` and post-window features. Early callback injection removes the registration gap but does not make this sequence safe. The asynchronous creation overload at [create_browser_window_non_android.cc][creation], lines 154 to 164, also posts a raw result pointer. Resolve an authoritative backend exclusion or a reviewed failure/readiness and cleanup contract, including these callers, then test the supported production path. The report accurately leaves this open at line 35.

2. **P1, already declared: owner deletion can invalidate the native callback's current stack before Views runs.** Patch line 60 enters unload; lines 174 to 179 guard only `OnWindowCloseComplete()` after its synchronous observer notification. A deleting observer can destroy Browser, its window and BrowserWidget before patch line 242 calls the Views base method. The stack-local weak completion at lines 52 to 58 can suppress a later deletion request, but cannot retain those objects or guarantee the required forced-close, Views-base, exact-owner-delete order. Retained [manager.cc][manager], lines 42 to 55 and 110 to 145, extracts entries and destroys them without an active-native-bracket ownership contract. Nested keyed-service `Shutdown()` remains part of the unresolved case. Views' own weak check at widget.cc (local uncommitted `arcified/native/patches/window_lifetime/inputs/ui/views/widget/widget.cc`), lines 2168 to 2184, protects its base continuation after `HandleWidgetDestroyed()`; it does not protect entry into that base method from the preceding forced-close call. Resolve ownership across the bracket and nested shutdown, or establish and test the applicable exclusion. The report accurately describes this limitation at lines 36 to 38.

3. **P2, already declared: ordinary deleting-observer continuations and lifecycle observations remain incomplete.** The new unload guard ends one local continuation. For example, retained [BrowserView][view], lines 4567 to 4596, returns from unload into Views' `CloseWithReason()`, whose widget.cc (local uncommitted `arcified/native/patches/window_lifetime/inputs/ui/views/widget/widget.cc`) implementation at lines 1035 to 1074 continues using widget state when close is permitted. Retained [TabStripModel][tabs], lines 1352 to 1366, holds a `ReentrancyCheck` through tab closure; its destructor at line 138 writes through the model's guard pointer. The weak check inside `CloseTabs()` at lines 3898 to 3900 does not establish the enclosing scope's lifetime. These are concrete caller obligations before claiming arbitrary deleting-observer safety, not newly introduced call paths. The draft tests cover repeated requests without owner deletion and same-target deletion after manager extraction. They do not settle these obligations. Complete this audit and the declared full feature/view ordering, session, keepalive and custom-window observations. The report already reserves these gates at lines 38, 57 and 76.

## Supported source changes

The nine-file patch changes seven production files and two existing browser-test files. In the two selected BrowserWidget destruction methods, BrowserView-based owner lookup, feature lookup, unload calls and manager calls are removed. The callbacks bind the exact constructing Browser at patch lines 29 to 39. The bundle passes through the existing factory into the widget constructor before native initialization. The retained-capture and local-patch search found the factory and Browser constructor callers updated by this patch; it does not establish repository-wide Chromium caller closure.

Both callbacks are move-only. The pre-view callback is moved out before owned-widget cleanup and runs after synchronous child `CloseNow()`, before derived-member/base teardown. Its unretained Browser binding remains available during `window_.reset()`. The full existing feature hook is called, including conditional `BrowserElementsViews::TearDown()` before animation and actor-border teardown at retained feature-source lines 1253 to 1257. The patch does not reconstruct that hook.

The native callback captures exact-owner weak completion before unload and moves completion to stack storage across the Views base call. There is no widget-member access after completion. Browser destructor entry revokes existing weak pointers and prevents new ones from being issued during teardown, while retaining pre-view cleanup. These are supported normal-path structures, subject to the gates above. Weak expiration alone is not a measurement of complete feature, session or keepalive destruction.

Manager production code and its unique ownership remain unchanged. Entry extraction precedes destruction; the member order at retained manager header lines 97 to 115 keeps subscriptions alive through Browser destruction. Direct `Shutdown()` still avoids `DeleteBrowser()`'s OTR, printing and application-exit side effects. Immutable Profile storage, regular-Profile/process keepalives, session placement, download checks, `features_.reset()` and the empty-tab assertion remain in place. Ordinary deletion remains posted. Recursive tab closing and its delete-scheduled guard remain unchanged.

The custom-window branch retains `CHECK_IS_TEST()` and creates no callback bundle. The WebUI factory branch drops its unused bundle without invoking it. Its retained destructor already calls feature pre-teardown before extensions/widget reset. No duplicate feature hook, owner, registry or replacement window is added. Runtime preservation of these paths is unverified.

## Test grounding and limits

The additions exercise production `CreateBrowserWindow()` or the real in-process `CreateBrowser()` helper, real native `CloseNow()`, ordinary window close, manager deletion/shutdown and modal-widget setup. There is no manual native-destroy invocation or callback-only simulation. The captured helper at `in_process_browser_test.cc:784` supplies a loaded tab and shows the Browser. The empty-window case uses the production factory directly.

Patch lines 314 to 344 observe close before Views' native-destruction observation, target liveness at that observation, target/widget weak expiration after `CloseNow()` and survival of the primary Browser. Lines 346 to 373 arrange posted deletion before native loss and repeat close requests. Lines 375 to 389 check ordinary return-before-deletion with the captured destruction waiter. Lines 391 to 428 use the captured non-Mac window-modal pattern and check that Profile and ExclusiveAccessManager remain accessible during child native destruction. The manager addition at lines 104 to 130 checks extraction and same-target reentry during direct shutdown; the existing OTR case gains weak-expiration observation at line 139.

These are useful source assertions. They have not been compiled, enumerated or run. The modal assertion observes one feature that the hook resets; it does not record the complete hook/view-removal sequence. The native observer runs inside Views handling, so it records a boundary within the base callback, not every base operation. Scheduling, selected Views/WebUI configuration and platform assumptions still need execution. No construction-loss or owner-deleting-bracket test is supplied. The feature report bounds these claims accurately.

Captured GN source confirms widget/view tests are direct sources of `//chrome/test:browser_tests` at `chrome/test/BUILD.gn:3469` and `:3470`, under `toolkit_views`. Manager tests are in `//chrome/browser/ui:browser_tests` at UI `BUILD.gn:4035`, consumed by the executable at test `BUILD.gn:1967`. No source-list edit is needed for these existing files. This is source registration, not GN evaluation or dependency checking.
